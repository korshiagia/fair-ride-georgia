# Georgia Ride-Hailing: Market, Regulatory and Economic Foundation

**Baseline cutoff:** 2026-10-03, Asia/Tbilisi; **public refresh:** 2026-10-04. The [latest evidence/decision update](PUBLIC-EVIDENCE-UPDATE-2026-10-04.md) and its market/legal/provider reports supersede specifically identified stale access limitations and provisional directions below; unrefreshed historical claims retain their original scope. Commercial eligibility, professional applicability and actual cohorts remain open. This is a planning evidence document, not a legal opinion or a market-size estimate. Source IDs refer to [SOURCE-REGISTER.md](SOURCE-REGISTER.md).

## 1. Decision implications

Build a transparent, reliable marketplace with measurable driver income and staffed safety operations. A lower commission is a testable part of that proposition; it cannot compensate for weak demand, long empty pickup distances, expensive vehicle finance, or an underfunded response team.

The release target includes **Zugdidi, Kutaisi, Batumi, Tbilisi and Rustavi**. Design a single platform with separate jurisdiction, city, service-area, airport and fare-policy configurations. Activate cities through explicit supply, legal, service-quality and financial gates. Production quality is required in every activated area; activation stages are operational risk management rather than disposable software stages.

The working economic proposal is a **transparent per-trip commission, with 15% as a modeling input**. This is neither a validated profitable tariff nor a published commission ceiling, and the commission base and gross/net VAT treatment remain open. Launch without driver subscriptions while demand remains unproven. Driver deductions, card/payout charges and mandatory service fees must be disclosed in the eventual approved offer.

Three verified findings materially affect the foundation:

- The 23.6% Bolt Georgia commission claim is supported by Bolt's current support page; it applies to cash/card fares and cancellation charges, with exclusions. [Bolt commission](https://bolt.eu/en-ge/support/articles/4405297960850/) (MR-01).
- On 2026-10-02, GCCA published a Yandex Go/Ridetech Georgia consumer-rights finding concerning information duties and unfair standard terms, with a one-month remedy period. This supports careful price disclosure and terms design; it does not prove that all competitor trips are unfair. [GCCA decision notice](https://gcca.gov.ge/index.php?cat_id=103&m=379) and [decision PDF](https://gcca.gov.ge/uploads_script/user_rights/tmp/phpaoYtaB.pdf) (MR-14, MR-15).
- Current privacy supervision belongs to the **State Audit Office** following the change effective 2026-03-02. Do not address new compliance procedures to the former Personal Data Protection Service. [Current personal-data law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307), [transition text](https://www.matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307?publication=7), [current NAPR rights notice](https://www.napr.gov.ge/en/single-page/processing-of-personal-data) (MR-10–12).

## 3. City and service activation policy

### Proposed sequence: Kutaisi → Zugdidi → Batumi → Tbilisi and Rustavi

This is a provisional execution choice for five-city release preparation, not a claim that competition is weak in any city. Kutaisi offers a bounded place to establish the operating model and investigate an airport-linked demand channel. Zugdidi tests resident demand without making airport demand the denominator. Batumi tests tourism-sensitive operations and seasonal exposure. Tbilisi/Rustavi form a later metropolitan/cross-boundary expansion once operations and municipal compliance are repeatable. Any city may move earlier if signed local supply and demand evidence are stronger. No launch date follows from this sequence.

GNTA's 2025 report gives Tbilisi 65.4%, Batumi 18.3% and Kutaisi 16.3% of international visitor visits entering by air. This supports treating airport channels as material research opportunities, but these visits are neither taxi trips nor all airport passenger movements. [GNTA 2025 overview](https://api.gnta.ge/storage/files/doc/statistical-data-overview-2025.pdf) (MR-21). Kutaisi's airport operator describes sustained growth; annual airport traffic cannot determine city-ride unit economics. [United Airports of Georgia](https://airports.ge/about-us/airports/kutaisi) (MR-22).

## 4. Licensing, driver verification and the platform's legal role

### Evidenced municipal requirements

**2026-10-04 national-framework correction:** the May2026 amendment №1584 extends the taxi permission clauses in the national Licences/Permits law to the capital **or another self-governing city**, effective **2026-05-29**. This does not transfer Tbilisi categories or establish each municipality's current implementing act. Zugdidi's city-versus-merged-municipality scope needs its own review; the 2017 merger and current code definitions are documented in the [legal refresh](PUBLIC-LEGAL-2026-10-04.md). [Current law](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/26824), [amendment](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/6870591).

Tbilisi Resolution 24-80 of 2018-07-27 is the base taxi instrument. The substantial 2023 amendment establishes A and B rules, an indefinite A-permit transition, and B application/vehicle requirements. A has white vehicle/roof-sign and displayed driver-card conditions; B is call-based, with no taxi identifying/illuminated sign. Vehicles are M1, left-hand drive with five doors; the rule also addresses inspection and the driver's valid licence. [Base instrument](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/4275377), [2023 amendment](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/5752819) (MR-17, MR-18).

**Unresolved current-law detail:** later amendments exist; the accessible base page does not expose a reliable fully amended annex in the extracted text. Counsel must obtain the final consolidated Georgian annex, including 2025 amendments and any changes after them. Do not hardcode permit fees, electric exemptions, bus-lane entitlements, duration, or colour/sign exceptions from a historical amendment. Portal access is not permission to automate a government registry. [Municipal taxi portal](https://taxi.tbilisi.gov.ge/) (MR-16).

**Our required controls, subject to lawful verification access:**

- Verify identity and valid driving entitlement; distinguish driving licence category B from taxi permit category B.
- Verify vehicle registration, owner/lessor authorization, inspection, permitted seat count, city permit/category and passenger-carrying insurance scope.
- Store verification evidence with source, reviewer, timestamp, expiry/recheck due date, permitted jurisdiction and revocation status. Unsupported documents lead to manual review rather than invented registry results.
- Determine lawful criminal-record checks, their relevance, consent/basis, retention and appeals before requesting records. A competitor's onboarding policy is not a government background-check mandate.
- Separate platform approval, fleet approval, driver approval, vehicle approval and municipal permission. Sharing an account or substituting an unapproved vehicle must not inherit approval.
- Recheck documentation and safety incidents through an accountable human process; urgent restrictions can be reversible pending investigation and appeal.

### Legal role must be selected before contracts and financial implementation

Counsel must compare intermediary/agency, carrier/operator, employer/contractor and fleet relationships **against actual conduct**: fare control, mandatory shifts, sanctions, account suspension, branding, exclusivity, service promises and insurance. Copying a competitor's “information service” text does not select the platform's lawful role. Public Defender material on platform work shows classification questions deserve review; it does not establish every taxi driver is an employee. [Public Defender report](https://www.ombudsman.ge/res/docs/2024052911382931838.pdf) (MR-29).

Deliver a signed issue matrix covering municipal versus national competence, cross-city rides, intercity permits if any, foreign driver/residency/right-to-work checks, consumer-facing carrier identity, insurance, employment, platform tax, driver tax, VAT, invoicing and cash receipts. Corporate/fleet contracts must specify who owns trip liability and who can see employee/driver location data.

## 5. Passenger rights, accessibility and safety commitments

The consumer law addresses pre-contract information in Georgian, price/calculation and additional charges, complaints, and discrimination. Applicability/exceptions must be reviewed for each contract type. [Consumer-rights law](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5420598) (MR-13).

The disability-rights law already contains private-entity accommodation duties. Its 2026-03-31 amendment, published 2026-04-03, adds website/app accessibility provisions effective **2027-09-01**, expressly covering passenger transport and third-party offer aggregation. Counsel must confirm scope and implementing technical regulations. [Disability-rights law, Articles 36 and 36¹](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/4923984) (MR-30).

**Product requirements chosen for this platform:** Georgian-first terms/support and interfaces, English passenger support, screen-reader flows, accessible fares and cancellation screens, non-map pickup alternatives, readable contrast and font scaling, text alternatives to voice contact, and accessible PIN assistance. Do not advertise wheelchair-accessible vehicles, child seats or a 24/7 live safety team unless that specific capability is staffed and verified in the active city.

Proof-of-arrival/geofence is evidence with uncertainty: GPS error, parallel roads, urban obstructions and spoofing must be considered. Never levy a no-show charge solely from a single client “arrived” tap or inaccurate geofence event. Require fresh location/accuracy signals, permitted safe pickup position, waiting-duration proof, notice to the passenger, and an appeal path. Design pickup PIN with a controlled assisted flow when a passenger has no battery/connectivity; record the reason without exposing the PIN in logs.

Safety requires incident severity definitions, trained coverage during all service hours, dispatching/escalation contacts, authenticated trip sharing, emergency-call support, evidence preservation with access audit, fraud review, complaint response and clear appeal rights. A risk engine's score is not proof of misconduct. Define human override and fair-review limits for deactivation, debt restrictions and cancellation sanctions. Validate local emergency arrangements before making response-time claims.

## 6. Privacy and international data architecture

**Legal baseline, to be translated into signed controls:** the current law treats location as personal data and criminal-record/identifying-biometric information as special-category data. It requires purpose/basis, minimization, necessary retention and security; it provides data-subject rights, conditional DPIA/DPO duties, processor agreements, and international-transfer conditions. Article 29 generally requires qualifying incident notice within 72 hours of identification, with its specified exception; processors notify controllers immediately. [Current law, Articles 3–6, 13–22, 27–29, 31, 33, 36–37](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/5827307) (MR-10).

**Our architecture proposals; numeric retention periods are not legal facts:**

| Data/use | Foundation decision and unresolved validation |
|---|---|
| Live driver location | Collect for online dispatch/active trips; distinguish driver offline, online-idle and on-trip. Restrict passenger access to the assigned vehicle while appropriate. Determine necessary freshness/history through DPIA and incident handling. |
| Passenger location | Explicit pickup pin can substitute for continuous tracking. Ask permission at use time; background permission needs a separate justified use. |
| Trip traces | Separate operational troubleshooting, safety evidence, pricing disputes and analytics purposes. Keep only justified granularity; exact routes must not leak to support/analytics exports by default. |
| Identity and eligibility documents | Store in restricted encrypted object storage; display verification outcomes rather than full documents to normal operations. Criminal records and biometric automation remain disabled until a basis and proportionality assessment are approved. |
| Corporate/fleet visibility | Roles and purpose restrictions; employers do not automatically receive employee live routes or personal-trip history. Owner changes revoke earlier tenant access. |
| AWS and SaaS processors | Region alone is insufficient: inventory AWS, Sentry, maps, messaging, push, fraud, support and analytics subprocessors, backups, support access and transfers. Counsel approves each mechanism and contract before real personal data. |
| Retention and erasure | Create a table with category, purpose, basis, retention trigger/period, legal hold, backup expiry and deletion mechanism. Use configurable schedules; approve actual periods against local tax, limitation, insurance and safety needs. |
| ML/risk | Prefer explainable policy rules initially. Limit consequential automation; provenance and human appeals. No unrelated reuse of traces to infer health, politics or sensitive venues. |
| Breach operation | Evidence clock, processor alerts, severity/rights assessment, regulator/subject notice decision, communications owner and incident register. Use current State Audit Office channels. |

DPIA, lawful-basis register, processor/transfer register, consent and notices, data-subject request workflow and DPO applicability decision are **required project gates** because of the product's own risk profile, even where the precise statutory trigger needs counsel review. Do not assume GDPR compliance alone settles Georgian requirements.

## 7. Payments, cash, payouts and accounting

The final Georgian payment law restricts payment-service activity to authorized categories and defines exclusions including direct cash and commercial-agent transactions. Its commercial-agent definition authorizes acting **only for the payer or only for the payee**. Whether this platform's actual flow qualifies must be determined before custody, wallet, collection or remittance functionality. The accessible English translation is explicitly a non-final 2015 version; do not use it as current authority. [Final Georgian payments law, Articles 2, 13–15](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/1673253) and [NBG provider registry](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/payment-system/providers) (MR-23, MR-24).

**Default design proposal:** use an approved Georgian bank/PSP as the regulated payment processor; avoid a stored-value wallet and passenger deposits initially. Map the exact merchant-of-record, payer, payee, fee invoice, driver beneficiary, refund and chargeback parties. Having a gateway API does not approve marketplace acquiring, driver submerchants or platform receipt of passenger money.

| Flow | Required behavior before launch |
|---|---|
| Passenger card | Provider-hosted/tokenized collection; no PAN/CVV storage. Verify lawful card-on-file/off-session authority, preauthorization/capture semantics, currencies and foreign-card acceptance with the contracted bank. |
| Cash trip | Driver receives fare; ledger records cash receivable/commission separately. Explain collection dates, negative balance, thresholds and appeal before restricting driver access. No pretend bank movement for cash. |
| Driver bank payout | Beneficiary verification, approved business purpose, payout schedule/cutoffs, failure/retry and reconciliation. Do not assume card acquiring automatically supplies mass payout APIs. |
| Refund/dispute | Separate passenger refund, driver adjustment, platform liability and provider reversal. Original entries remain; adjustments reference evidence and authority. |
| Corporate credit | Credit underwriting, deposit/limit if lawful, contractual billing party, approved employee trips and invoice/payment matching. Corporate credit is not passenger stored-value. |
| Fleet | Contractual beneficiary and driver-level allocation, tenant access, owner changes, tax/statement responsibility and dispute ownership. |

## 8. Driver and platform economics to validate

### Required measures

Measure per **online hour** and by city/shift/vehicle ownership, not only per completed ride. Online time includes waiting, pickup, trip and repositioning while available; retain a separate engaged-hour measure. Ask for comparable multi-app working-time allocation rather than double-counting the same hour.

Driver economic net = fares received + funded bonuses + tips − platform deductions − fuel/charging for all working distance − maintenance/tyres for all working distance − allocated insurance/inspection − rent or approved finance/depreciation allocation − applicable tax. Allocate pickup, repositioning and empty-return distance into those cost buckets once; do not deduct an additional empty-distance charge for the same fuel or maintenance. Unpaid working time belongs in the online-hour denominator. Avoid counting loan principal and depreciation as the same economic cost. Report net before and after capital allocation/tax separately when inputs differ in reliability. A high ride count with expensive idle/repositioning time can still be unattractive.

Platform contribution = collected commission and explicit permitted fees + corporate revenue − VAT attributable to those revenues − acquiring/payout costs − funded discounts/bonuses − variable maps/messaging/cloud costs − variable support/safety/insurance allocation − fraud/refund/chargeback loss. Report contribution before and after customer acquisition; include fixed operations and engineering for break-even/runway. Passenger fare collected as agent is not automatically platform revenue.

### Illustrative arithmetic only

For a hypothetical GEL 20 commission-eligible fare, a modeled 15% deduction is GEL 3 and leaves GEL 17 before the driver's costs/taxes. Holding fare and eligibility identical, a 23.6% deduction would be GEL 4.72, a GEL 1.72 difference. **This does not predict earnings or competitor net income, establish a published driver rate, or resolve the commission tax base.** If the GEL 3 fee were VAT-inclusive at an illustrative 18% under the eventual approved treatment, revenue before VAT would be approximately GEL 2.54, before processing or safety costs. That conditional sensitivity is not a platform tax determination; do not apply both VAT-inclusive and VAT-exclusive deductions in the same scenario.

Commission, fare floors, surge caps and bonuses must be validated jointly. A 15% fee with inadequate matching density may produce worse driver net per hour than a higher fee with more paid work. Driver choice is earned through reliable demand and fair handling, not commission alone.

## 10. Mapping procurement and data licensing

Google's current core coverage table lists Georgian geocoding, traffic layer, driving/snap-to-road and walking features, but speed-limit and cycling coverage is limited/unavailable by its legend. Country-level availability is not validation of entrances, Georgian addresses, routing or ETA quality. [Google coverage](https://developers.google.com/maps/coverage) (MR-31).

Mapbox offers routing, traffic-aware profiles and map matching; traffic routing can fall back where traffic data is absent. Georgian traffic/search quality and contract limits remain to be tested. [Mapbox Directions](https://docs.mapbox.com/api/navigation/directions/) (MR-32).

OpenStreetMap data and the public OSM tile service are different procurement choices: the public tile policy does not supply a commercial SLA and restricts usage. Select a contracted tile/geocoding/routing provider or a funded self-hosted stack with licensing/attribution review. [OSM tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/) (MR-33). No Georgian local-map vendor with verified ride-hailing API, SLA and quotation was established here; local candidates can be researched without claiming a signed option.

Keep the platform's own permitted-pickup geometry, terminal meeting points and arrival evidence independent of proprietary vendor fields. Review storage/caching/derived-data/attribution restrictions before persisting vendor routes or combining maps products. Do not promise free production mapping or equal behavior from interchangeable providers.
